Foundations: from Newton to an operator

Part 0 of Instruments as Operators

Series · 0 Foundations · 1 Violin · 2 Clarinet · 3 Kalimba · 4 Piano · 5 Drum

Build a string out of beads, apply Newton’s law to one bead, and let the beads crowd together. The wave equation falls out, and so does the reason a string’s overtones are whole numbers.

One bead, two pulls

  1. Put N beads of mass m on a massless thread with tension T, spaced h apart, with both ends tied down. Let u_j be the height of bead j, and set u_0 = u_{N+1} = 0 for the tied ends.
  2. The thread on the right pulls bead j along itself with force T. For small slopes its vertical part is T\sin\theta \approx T\tan\theta = T(u_{j+1}-u_j)/h. The thread on the left contributes T(u_{j-1}-u_j)/h.
  3. Newton’s second law for bead j is therefore m\,\ddot u_j = \frac{T}{h}\left(u_{j+1} - 2u_j + u_{j-1}\right).

The right side is a second difference: it measures how far bead j sits below the average of its neighbors. Drag a bead and watch the arrows.

Arrows show the net vertical force on each bead, rescaled to stay visible as N grows. Drag any bead, then release.

Crowd the beads together

  1. Fix the total length L = (N+1)h and the mass per length \mu = m/h. Dividing the bead equation by m = \mu h gives \ddot u_j = \frac{T}{\mu}\cdot\frac{u_{j+1} - 2u_j + u_{j-1}}{h^2}.
  2. As h \to 0 the second difference divided by h^2 tends to u_{xx}, by Taylor’s theorem. With c^2 = T/\mu this is the wave equation.
TipThe string’s operator

u_{tt} = -\mathcal{L}u, \qquad \mathcal{L} = -c^2\partial_x^2, \qquad u(0) = u(L) = 0 .

The bead chain also has an exact spectrum. Its frequencies are \omega_n = 2\sqrt{T/mh}\,\sin\big(n\pi/2(N+1)\big) for n = 1,\dots,N. Slide N up: for small n the sine is nearly linear, and the stems land on the integer grid.

Spectrum of the bead chain

Gray lines are whole-number multiples of the lowest frequency. Stem height is how strongly the current shape excites each mode.

The assumptions were small slopes, tension unchanged by stretching, no bending stiffness and no air. Each later instrument relaxes one of them.

The recipe for every instrument

Each part repeats two moves. The operator is the restoring force per unit mass, written as a function of the shape: tension gives -c^2\partial_x^2, bending gives \kappa^2\partial_x^4, tension in a sheet gives -c^2\Delta. The boundary conditions record what each support forbids.

Support What it forbids Condition Where
tied end moving u = 0 violin
clamp moving and tilting u = 0,\ u' = 0 kalimba base
free tip bending moment and shear force u'' = 0,\ u''' = 0 kalimba tip
pin moving and bending moment u = 0,\ u'' = 0 piano, idealized
rim moving u = 0 on the circle drum
open end of a pipe pressure above room pressure p = 0 clarinet bell, first open hole
reed, nearly shut air moving p_x = 0 clarinet mouthpiece

Then the spectrum of the operator fixes the frequencies, and the way the instrument is played fixes how much of each you hear.
