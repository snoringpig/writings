The violin

Part 1 of Instruments as Operators

The string from Part 0, played two ways. Tension and length set the pitch; the bow forces the overtones onto exact whole numbers.

Operator and spectrum

A violin string is the continuum bead chain of Part 0, so its operator is -c^2\partial_x^2 on [0,L] with tied ends. Its eigenfunctions are \sin(n\pi x/L), and the frequencies are f_n = \frac{n}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots

The four strings share one length and differ in T/\mu, tuned to G3, D4, A4 and E5. A finger shortens the vibrating length, and since f \propto 1/L, stopping the string at a fraction s of its length raises the pitch by a factor 1/(1-s). That is why the semitone markers below bunch together toward the bridge.

Play it

Press and hold a string. Slide left and right to finger it.

Left edge is the nut, right edge the bridge. The ticks mark equal-tempered semitones, at fractions 1 − 2−k/12 of the length. The stems are the partials you hear. The body is a linear filter, so switching it off or on changes their heights, never their positions. Its resonances here (about 280, 470, 550 and 2700 Hz) are approximate textbook values.

What the bow does

The bow grips the string and drags it along (stick) until the string’s tension tears it free and it flies back (slip). Helmholtz watched this through a vibration microscope and described the result in 1863: the string becomes two straight segments joined by one sharp corner, and the corner races around a lens-shaped path, once per period. In modal terms that shape is u(x,t) = \sum_{n\ge1} \frac{A}{n^2}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,\sin(n\omega_1 t).

The motion repeats exactly every period, so its spectrum sits exactly on n f_1, and the force on the bridge falls off like 1/n. A real string is slightly stiff and its own frequencies run a little sharp (Part 4); the stick–slip cycle locks every partial to the period anyway. A pluck, by contrast, lets each mode ring freely at its own frequency and decay.

Slowed about 500 times. The faint lens is the envelope the corner traces. Stems: partials of the bridge force.

The body

The string barely moves any air. It rocks the bridge, which drives the wooden body, which radiates. Each link is linear, so it can change how loud each partial is but never move one. Toggle the body above while playing: the stems change height and stay put.
