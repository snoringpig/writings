The string’s operator in two dimensions. The zeros of Bessel functions replace the whole numbers, and where you strike decides which modes speak.

1.00
Tap the head anywhere. The ring marks where we listen.

The motion is the real superposition of the 40 lowest modes, slowed about 300 times. Strike dead center: only round modes appear. Strike on a nodal line of a mode and that mode stays silent.

Deriving the operator

  1. A drumhead is a sheet under tension T, measured as force per unit length of any cut through it, with mass \sigma per unit area. Take a small patch D of it.
  2. Along each piece ds of the patch’s edge, the outside sheet pulls with force T\,ds along the surface. For small slopes its vertical part is T\,\partial_n u\,ds, where \partial_n u is the slope in the outward direction.
  3. Adding these up and applying the divergence theorem gives the net vertical force, T\oint_{\partial D} \partial_n u\,ds = T\iint_D \Delta u\,dA .
  4. Newton’s law for the patch is \iint_D \sigma u_{tt}\,dA = T\iint_D \Delta u\,dA. This holds for every patch, so the integrands agree.
TipThe drumhead’s operator

u_{tt} = -\mathcal{L}u,\qquad \mathcal{L} = -c^2\Delta,\qquad c^2 = T/\sigma,\qquad u = 0 \text{ on the rim}.

In one dimension the patch is a segment, its edge is two endpoints, and step 3 is the fundamental theorem of calculus. That is Part 0’s equation again.

Tension pulls outward all around the edge. Where the sheet bulges, the pulls tilt and their vertical parts add up to T times the Laplacian of u, per unit area.

Spectrum

Separating variables in polar coordinates gives standing waves J_m(kr)\cos m\theta and J_m(kr)\sin m\theta, with J_m a Bessel function; the second solution of Bessel’s equation blows up at the center and is discarded. The rim condition is J_m(ka) = 0, so f_{m,n} = \frac{c\,j_{m,n}}{2\pi a}, where j_{m,n} is the n-th zero of J_m. These zeros are not evenly spaced and no common base frequency fits them, so the drum has no clear pitch. Each frequency with m \ge 1 belongs to two modes, \cos m\theta and \sin m\theta: the round head’s symmetry, visible in the spectrum.

A strike at p, heard at q, gives mode k the weight X_k(p)X_k(q)/\lVert X_k\rVert^2, the eigen-expansion of a point kick. Tap a stem to see one mode on the drumhead and hear it alone.
